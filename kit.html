Field Kit for student researchers

The atlas only grows through careful first-hand work. This kit walks you from a gap on the map to a record a custodian has agreed to share. Nothing here replaces your university's ethics process or the wishes of the people whose place it is.

Rule zero. The place belongs to its custodians, not to the atlas. Ask first, accept a no, and never publish what you were not given permission to publish.

1. The research path

  1. Pick a gap. Open a profile and read "What we still don't know", or use the coverage list on the map page to find the constituencies with the fewest profiles.
  2. Check what is required. Confirm your university ethics approval and whether any national or provincial research permission applies. This prototype has not verified those requirements, so ask your lecturer and the Malaita Provincial Government.
  3. Go through the right door. Approach chiefs, the association or the site committee first, ideally through someone they trust. Explain what you want to record, who will see it, and that they can say no or withdraw at any point.
  4. Record consent and its scope. What may be shown, to whom, for how long. Use the consent record template below.
  5. Log every source. Interviews, documents and observations each get a row in the source register. Separate what you saw from what you were told from what you read.
  6. Handle location carefully. Take true coordinates only with permission, keep them in a private file, and publish a generalised point or none.
  7. Hand back for review. Show the draft profile to the custodians before anyone else sees it. Record who reviewed it and when.

    4. Research questions for tourism students

    Conservation sites and tourism pull in different directions. These questions help you document both sides without promoting a place that is not ready or willing.

    ThemeQuestions worth asking (with permission)
    Access and protocolWho gives permission to visit? Are there fees, guides or kastom rules? Are there times or places that are off limits?
    CapacityWhere could visitors sleep, eat and get water? How would they reach the site? What happens in an emergency, with limited signal?
    BenefitsWho earns from visitors now? Who would like to? How are decisions about money made?
    Pressure and impactWhat would more visitors change for fishing, forest, waste and water? What numbers could the place handle?
    CultureWhich stories, practices or sites should not be shared with outsiders? Who decides, and how should that be shown on the atlas?
    Wants and worriesDoes the community want to be listed at all? What would make a listing useful, and what would make it harmful?
    ComparisonHow do other community-run places in Solomon Islands manage visitors and conservation together? Compare what worked and what did not.
