Field Kit for student researchers

The atlas only grows through careful first-hand work. This kit walks you from a gap on the map to a record a custodian has agreed to share. Nothing here replaces your university's ethics process or the wishes of the people whose place it is.

Rule zero. The place belongs to its custodians, not to the atlas. Ask first, accept a no, and never publish what you were not given permission to publish.

1. The research path

  1. Pick a gap. Open a profile and read "What we still don't know", or use the coverage list on the map page to find the constituencies with the fewest profiles.
  2. Check what is required. Confirm your university ethics approval and whether any national or provincial research permission applies. This prototype has not verified those requirements, so ask your lecturer and the Malaita Provincial Government.
  3. Go through the right door. Approach chiefs, the association or the site committee first, ideally through someone they trust. Explain what you want to record, who will see it, and that they can say no or withdraw at any point.
  4. Record consent and its scope. What may be shown, to whom, for how long. Use the consent record template below.
  5. Log every source. Interviews, documents and observations each get a row in the source register. Separate what you saw from what you were told from what you read.
  6. Handle location carefully. Take true coordinates only with permission, keep them in a private file, and publish a generalised point or none.
  7. Hand back for review. Show the draft profile to the custodians before anyone else sees it. Record who reviewed it and when.

    4. Research questions for tourism students

    Conservation sites and tourism pull in different directions. These questions help you document both sides without promoting a place that is not ready or willing.

    ThemeQuestions worth asking (with permission)
    Access and protocolWho gives permission to visit? Are there fees, guides or kastom rules? Are there times or places that are off limits?
    CapacityWhere could visitors sleep, eat and get water? How would they reach the site? What happens in an emergency, with limited signal?
    BenefitsWho earns from visitors now? Who would like to? How are decisions about money made?
    Pressure and impactWhat would more visitors change for fishing, forest, waste and water? What numbers could the place handle?
    CultureWhich stories, practices or sites should not be shared with outsiders? Who decides, and how should that be shown on the atlas?
    Wants and worriesDoes the community want to be listed at all? What would make a listing useful, and what would make it harmful?
    ComparisonHow do other community-run places in Solomon Islands manage visitors and conservation together? Compare what worked and what did not.

    6. How to publish an update

    Only the atlas maintainer publishes. Custodians, students and anyone else can ask for an update: . The maintainer adds an entry to data/updates.json, checks it and uploads the file. There is no form, server or account.

    Two kinds of update

    • Primary update: needs a source in data/sources.json from an organisation marked trusted: true (a government, provincial, custodian or NGO source), and a consent record ID. One that fails either check shows as a community note marked Needs checking.
    • Community note, unverified: from anyone. Always shown with that label.

    Checklist before uploading

    • Consent: confirm the consent record exists before adding a primary entry. Only its ID (for example C01) goes in the file. The record itself stays private.
    • Source: add the source to data/sources.json first, with its authority_type and organisation.
    • No coordinates: never write coordinates for any place. An entry containing coordinates is hidden on every page until they are removed.
    • Date: write YYYY-MM-DD. Pages show it as “as of” that date.
    • Weight (1 to 5): sets how long an update can stay on the homepage without being pinned: about 79 days at 5, 60 at 4 and 35 at 3. At 2 it only appears on the day it is dated, and at 1 never. Use "pinned": true for something that must stay; pinned updates still count toward the three homepage slots.
    • Check: Open updates-check.html locally and clear every error before uploading. Hidden entries are still readable in data/updates.json.
    • Withdrawing: set "status": "withdrawn" and a withdrawn_reason (consent, sensitivity or other). For consent or sensitivity, also empty the title, summary and body. Git history still keeps earlier versions of the file, so get consent in writing before publishing a custodian’s story.

    Entry template

    Copy into the updates list in data/updates.json. For a community note, set tier to "secondary" and delete the consent_id line.

    {
  "id": "U-0001",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "title": "",
  "summary": "",
  "body": "",
  "tier": "primary",
  "source_ids": ["S00"],
  "consent_id": "C01",
  "site_ids": ["SM-001"],
  "weight": 3,
  "pinned": false,
  "status": "published"
}

    7. How to submit research

    Researchers can offer published or unpublished work about East Are’are, West Are’are or Small Malaita for the research library. The atlas maintainer lists it by adding an entry to data/sources.json, the same register that holds the atlas’s first sources. To submit research: .

    Listing is not endorsement. A listed work is about this area and its author agreed to be listed. The atlas has not checked its methods or findings.

    Not for the library. Traditional ecological knowledge, oral histories and anything from custodians do not belong here. They are handled through consent and, where the custodians agree, as primary updates (section 6).

    Checklist before listing

    • Rights: the author agrees in writing to be listed. Keep that agreement private; only "rights_confirmed": true goes in the file. Without it the entry stays hidden.
    • Link out: url points to the published page or DOI. No full texts, PDFs, photographs or videos are stored in this repository.
    • Summary: in your own words, 80 words at most. Never copy an abstract. Leave it out if unsure; the entry then shows its citation and link only.
    • No locations: never copy coordinates or a site location from a paper. An entry whose title, summary or citation contains coordinates is hidden everywhere.
    • Scope: region_tags needs at least one site ID (for example SM-001) or one of the three constituency names.
    • Evidence kind: one of .
    • Taxon groups: any of .
    • Check: open updates-check.html locally and clear every error before uploading.

    8. Add a photo or video

    Students prepare an entry here; only Aziel adds entries to data/media.json. Pick each item by hand. The form never visits the address you type, and nothing leaves your browser.

    Before you start. Only material posted publicly by an organisation, or our own photos. Never a post from a person’s own account. No identifiable children: if you are unsure of someone’s age, leave the item out. Identifiable adults only with a consent record. Never record a location, and never take a place from a post’s geotag.

    • Posts by organisations (government, intergovernmental bodies, NGOs, community organisations, news outlets, including posts by their staff or page admins on their behalf): record who posted it and the address. Do not download or copy them. Posts from a person’s own account cannot be used.
    • Your own photos, or ones given to us with permission: never from a social media account. Run the file through “Prepare a photo”, put only the cleaned copy in the team folder and paste its link. They are never embedded, and photos and videos are never stored in this repository.
    • Video: never held. YouTube, Facebook and Vimeo videos posted by organisations can be loaded on click; others link out.
