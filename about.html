About and method

Status. This is a teaching prototype. The five profiles were compiled on 19 October 2026 from public web sources only. No custodian, tribe, association or government office has reviewed them, and several are single-source. Do not cite them as official records. Please do not share this link widely until custodians have been asked.

What this atlas is for

It maps conservation sites in East Are'are, West Are'are and Small Malaita and shows, for each, what is known, how we know it, and what is still missing. It is built so that students and community members can add careful, sourced, permitted information over time.

What the verification labels mean

LabelMeaning
Draft (single source)Compiled from one published source. Treat as a lead, not a fact.
Desk-sourcedCompiled from two or more published sources. Nobody connected with the site has reviewed it.
Community-checkedNamed custodians reviewed the profile, and the review (who, when, what scope) is recorded. This does not mean everyone in the community agrees.
Source-verifiedKey facts checked against primary records such as a certificate, charter or management plan.
DisputedCustodians or sources disagree. Details may be hidden while this is worked out.

Each fact also carries a confidence tag: Reported (a source says so), Corroborated (independent sources agree), Conflicting (sources disagree) or Unknown.

What counts as a site

Conservation in Solomon Islands mostly happens on customary land, so a legal designation is only one kind of site. The atlas separates three kinds so that nobody mistakes a community commitment for a legal protection:

  • Legally declared protected area: registered under the Protected Areas Act 2010. Kira is the one example found so far.
  • Community-managed area: a defined area with community rules, such as a taboo or no-take zone, without national registration.
  • Community conservation group: an association or initiative that works on conservation, where the area it looks after is not yet defined in published sources.

Key Biodiversity Areas are scientific designations, not community sites, and are noted only for context.

Considered but outside the area

These came up in research but are not in East Are'are, West Are'are or Small Malaita, so they are not profiled:

  • Manaoba Locally Managed Marine Area, Lau Lagoon (north-east Malaita) [S07].
  • Kwainaa'isi, Kafarumu and Aifasu, Kwaio highlands, mapped by the Baru Conservation Alliance, about 5.7 km² combined [S11].
  • Key Biodiversity Areas listed for Malaita Province: Langa Langa Lagoon, Lau Lagoon and Ndai [S10].

What this suggests. In the searches run, no public resource combined a local map, per-claim sources, consent status and visitor protocols for Are'are or Small Malaita, and no site was found for Small Malaita or West Are'are at all. Absence from web search is not absence on the ground: SPREP, the Environment and Conservation Division, Malaita Provincial Government, WorldFish, SICAN and local associations may hold material that is not online. Ask them before assuming a gap.

Sensitive information

  • Sacred, taboo or tribal-secret places, and locations of threatened species, are generalised or withheld. Where a source mentions taboo sites (as at Puriasi), this atlas does not expand on them.
  • True coordinates of sensitive places must never be committed to the public repository, because version history keeps them forever. Keep them in a private register.
  • Named individuals in sources (officials, coordinators, project officers) are not republished unless they agree to be listed.
  • Raw interviews, genealogies and community-held documents stay out of the public repository.
  • "Visitor access" defaults to Unknown - ask first. Publishing a place can increase visitor pressure, so a pin is never an invitation.

Corrections and removal

In a real release this section would name a monitored contact. For this prototype there is none yet. The intended process: anyone can ask for a correction, and a custodian can ask for any profile or detail to be hidden at once while the request is considered. Hidden does not mean deleted from history, which is why sensitive details are never committed in the first place.

Known limitations of this prototype

  • Only one site has a map point, and it is a bay-level point rather than a boundary.
  • No constituency boundaries are drawn. A permitted, current boundary file has not been confirmed.
  • No photographs, because no permissions exist yet.
  • No Solomon Pijin or Are'are-language summaries. These should be written by local reviewers.
  • Several sources are news articles or funder profiles, some from 2018-2022.
